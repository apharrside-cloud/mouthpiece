Mouthpiece is free voice typing for Android. Speech is turned into text on your phone, so there is no account, no subscription and nothing uploaded.

What it does

Works everywhere

A small bubble floats by the text field you are in: messages, email, notes, browser, anything with a keyboard.

Private by design

The speech model runs on your phone. Your voice never leaves it. The app only downloads the speech model, once, and peeks at a tiny version file to tell you about updates.

Cleans up as you talk

Removes “um”, adds punctuation and capitals, and turns “twenty five dollars” into $25 and “dot com” into .com. In an email box, “sam at gmail dot com” becomes sam@gmail.com; phone and number boxes get plain digits.

Fixes mistakes by voice

Say “scratch that” or “no wait” to redo the last sentence. Say “all caps”, “capitalize that”, “lowercase that” or “replace noon with one” to change what you just said. Tap Undo if it got something wrong.

Learns your words

Correct a name once and it offers to remember it. Set a different writing style for each app.

Tap or hold

Tap to start and tap to stop, or press and hold like a walkie-talkie. Pick your bubble shape (tall pill, circle or square), its symbol, and a light or dark theme. Add the Quick Settings tile to switch the bubble on or off, or drag it to the bottom of the screen to hide it for 10 minutes.

Install it (about 3 minutes)

  1. Download and open the fileTap the button above, then open the downloaded mouthpiece-__VERSION__.apk. If Android asks, allow installs from your browser, then tap Install. If Play Protect warns that the app is unrecognized, choose “More details” then “Install anyway” (it is not on the Play Store yet).
  2. Open Mouthpiece and follow “Finish setup”Allow the microphone when asked.
  3. Tap Mouthpiece in Accessibility (it will say “restricted”)Open Settings → Accessibility, find Mouthpiece (under Installed apps or Downloaded apps) and tap its name. Android shows a “Restricted setting” message; tap OK. This is expected, and it is what makes the menu in the next step appear.
  4. Allow restricted settingsOpen Settings → Apps → Mouthpiece (or press and hold the app icon → App info), tap the ⋮ menu at the top right, and choose Allow restricted settings. Confirm with your fingerprint or PIN if asked.
  5. Turn on the accessibility serviceGo back to Settings → Accessibility → Mouthpiece and switch it On. This is what lets the bubble type into other apps. See “Why accessibility?” below.
  6. Download the speech modelBack in the app, pick a model and let it download on Wi-Fi. Balanced (190 MB) is a good start, Fast (60 MB) suits older phones, and the larger ones are more accurate but slower. You can switch or remove models later. This is the main time the app uses the internet.
  7. DictateTap any text field, tap the orange bubble, speak, and tap it again. Or press and hold, talk, and let go.

Tip: when the app asks to be exempt from battery optimization, say yes. Otherwise Android may stop the bubble in the background.

Questions

Is it really private?

Audio is recorded only while the bubble is listening and is transcribed on your phone by an open-source Whisper model. The app has no accounts and no analytics. Its only network use is downloading the model file from Hugging Face the first time, and reading this site's version file about twice a day to tell you when an update is out (you can turn that off in Settings). Nothing about you or your phone is sent. Dictation history stays on your phone, and you can turn it off.

Why does it need accessibility?

Android has no other way for a voice app to type into any other app. The service reads the text field you tapped so it can place the bubble next to it, insert your words, and notice when you correct a word. It does not record your screen and it sends nothing anywhere. You can turn it off any time in Settings → Accessibility.

Which phones work?

Android 12 or newer on a 64-bit ARM phone, which covers almost every phone since about 2018. About 400 MB of free space is needed. It was built and tested on a Pixel 9 Pro XL, where an 11-second clip is transcribed in about 4 seconds. Older or cheaper phones are slower: choose the Fast model in Settings.

It stopped working after a while

Open the app. If it says setup is unfinished, Android may have turned off the accessibility service or limited the app in the background. Turn the service back on and allow it to run without battery restrictions.

How do I update or remove it?

To update, download the new file from this page and install it over the old one; your words and settings are kept. To remove it, uninstall it like any other app (turn off the accessibility service first if you like).

Something is wrong or missing

It is an early beta and your feedback is the point. In the app, open Home, scroll to Feedback and tap Report a problem or Suggest a feature. Say what happened, which app you were in, and what you said. Only what you type, plus the app version and your phone model, is sent.
